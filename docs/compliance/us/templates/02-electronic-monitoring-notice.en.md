## Drafting notes by state

### New York — N.Y. Civ. Rights Law § 52-c

Status: in force. An employer with a place of business in New York that monitors telephone communications or transmissions, electronic mail or transmissions, or internet access or usage by electronic device or system must:

- give each newly hired employee prior written notice upon hiring;
- obtain written or electronic acknowledgment;
- advise that covered activity may be monitored “at any and all times and by any lawful means”; and
- post the notice in a conspicuous place readily available for viewing by affected employees.

The one-time 90-day notice period for employees already employed when the law took effect has passed. The Attorney General enforces; statutory maximum civil penalties are $500 for a first violation, $1,000 for a second, and $3,000 for each third or subsequent violation. The statute says it does not create a private right of action. [Official bill and text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2021/A430).

### Connecticut — Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-48d

Status: in force. Before monitoring, an employer must give prior written notice to all employees who may be affected, stating the types of monitoring that may occur. A notice posted in a conspicuous place readily available for viewing by employees constitutes prior written notice. The statute permits monitoring without prior notice where the employer has reasonable grounds to believe employees are violating law, violating the employer's or employees' legal rights, or creating a hostile workplace environment, and monitoring may produce evidence. Use that exception only with counsel. The Labor Commissioner may impose statutory civil penalties. The statute does not require an acknowledgment, but collecting one supplies evidence of delivery. [Official statute](https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_557.htm#sec_31-48d).

### Delaware — Del. Code tit. 19, § 705

Status: in force. Before or at monitoring, an employer must either:

- give an electronic notice of monitoring each day the employee accesses the employer-provided email or internet-access services, acknowledged by the employee; or
- give a one-time written or electronic notice of monitoring, acknowledged in writing or electronically.

The Department of Labor may assess a $100 civil penalty for each violation. The section does not apply to processes performed solely for computer-system maintenance/protection when not targeted to monitor a particular individual's email or internet activity. [Official statute](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title19/c007/sc01/index.html#705).

### Multi-state operation

This combined notice uses the strict elements confirmed above, but no single form resolves every jurisdiction. Counsel should assess state wiretap/recording laws, biometric and location laws, union/works-council or collective-bargaining duties, off-duty conduct, common-law privacy, local ordinances, and whether consent must be voluntary. Reissue the notice before materially expanding data types, purposes, recipients, or monitoring methods.
